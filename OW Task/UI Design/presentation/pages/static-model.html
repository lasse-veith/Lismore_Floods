How the Data Was Used

Every property starts from a real address in the G-NAF register, filtered down to Lismore, South Lismore, East Lismore, Goonellabah, Girards Hill and Loftville. That's what gives each property its real latitude and longitude, so the whole portfolio sits on genuine ground rather than made-up points scattered around a suburb.

Once a property has real coordinates, the 1m LiDAR DEM gives it a real ground elevation. This is the same 2010 survey that fed into the actual 2022 damage assessment, so the elevation values here line up with what was used to judge real flood impact at the time, not a rough estimate pulled from a different source. Combined with the real Wilsons River water levels from that damage assessment, this is what determines exactly how deep the water got at each address, hour by hour through the event.

Floor area matters because it drives flooring, painting and demolition costs. Where OpenStreetMap has a building footprint traced for a property (around 3,000 of them), that real footprint is used directly. For the remaining properties, floor area is instead drawn from the 2021 Census GCP DataPacks for the area, using the real distribution of dwelling sizes and types reported for Lismore rather than a single average figure applied to everyone.

The Census data does double duty beyond floor area. It also calibrates the mix of construction types, building ages and dwelling structures generated for the synthetic side of the model, so even the parts that aren't measured directly for each property still reflect what's actually typical for housing in this area.
